I went to Quantic School of Business and Technology’s Global Conference in London expecting to learn how to use AI better.

I left realizing we may be asking the wrong question entirely.

Not:

How can I use AI better?

But:

What should we redesign entirely?

The Illusion of “Using AI Better”

At the conference, we built real applications in minutes.

Meme generators.
Networking matchmakers.
Even badge-to-LinkedIn scanners.

One of them (slightly addictive 😄):
👉 https://lnkd.in/e3vN4weC

It was fun. It was impressive.

But that’s not the story.

The Real Shift

What became clear very quickly:

AI is no longer just a tool for individuals.
It’s a force for redesigning entire workflows.

We’ve already optimized for speed:

writing faster
coding faster
thinking faster

But organizations don’t transform because individuals get faster.

They transform when:

processes are redesigned
decisions become system-driven and smarter
repetitive work disappears into automation

So humans can focus on what actually requires judgment.

From Productivity to System Design

One example that stuck with me was a case study from Unilever.

They applied AI to hiring:

~1.8 million applications processed
time-to-hire reduced from 4 months → 4 weeks
candidate acceptance rates improved

At scale.

That’s not productivity.

That’s system redesign.

The New Bottleneck

Here’s the uncomfortable truth:

AI capability is no longer the constraint.

The real constraint is:

people who can apply it in the right context
people who exercise judgment
people who take accountability for outcomes
Leadership Is Quietly Being Redefined

The shift isn’t toward leaders who “use AI tools.”

It’s toward leaders who:

re-architect systems
understand where AI fits (and where it doesn’t)
intentionally keep humans in the loop where judgment matters

This is a very different skill set.

And a much higher bar.

A New Standard

One insight from the workshop stayed with me:

We can now build apps in ~30 minutes.

Which means the question is no longer:

Can you build it?

It’s:

Should this exist at all?

Final Thought

We’re at a point where optimizing tasks is table stakes.

The real opportunity—and responsibility—is redesigning systems.

Curious how others are thinking about this:

Are you still optimizing tasks with AI…
or already redesigning workflows?
